Lesson
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What does your best work, seen together, say about you as an artist and about what you have learned?
You have made digital art all year: pixels and layers, color and shape, photography, collage, typography, drawing, and animation. None of that work has been seen together, side by side, as one body of work. In this module you are not making something new. You are choosing, arranging, and presenting the pieces that best show who you have become as a digital artist.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

A portfolio is a curated collection of your strongest work, arranged with intention so a viewer can follow it easily. Think back across this whole year of digital art. Which piece are you proudest of, and why?
📚 PART 2 · CHOOSING AND ORDERING YOUR WORK

Selection is choosing which pieces go in. You have produced work in nearly every module this year: imaging foundations, elements and principles, color, shape and editing tools, photography, collage, typography, drawing fundamentals, and animation. A strong portfolio does not include all of it.

  • Choose 6 to 10 pieces, not everything you made.
  • Pick quality over completeness. One strong piece says more than three weak ones.
  • It is fine, and expected, to leave out your earliest or weakest work. Growth means your later work should outshine your first attempts.

Once you have your pieces, sequencing is the order you place them in. A portfolio is not a random dump of files; the order tells a story about you as an artist.

Chronological order

Arrange pieces in the order you made them, earliest to latest. This shows growth directly: a viewer watches your skill develop from your first module to your last.

Grouped by medium or theme

Cluster related pieces, for example all photography together, then all collage. This shows range: a viewer sees the breadth of techniques you can work in.

Either order is valid. What matters is that you pick one on purpose and stay consistent, rather than placing pieces in whatever order the files happen to be saved.

💡 HOW TO CHOOSE WHAT STAYS IN
For each candidate piece, ask one question: "Does this show a skill or idea I want a viewer to know I can do?" If yes, it is a strong candidate. If the honest answer is "I just do not want to delete it," leave it out. Go back through your portfolio folder from your first module to Module 31 and shortlist before you commit to a final six to ten.
🔍 PART 3 · CAPTIONING AND PRESENTATION

Every piece needs a short caption: a few sentences that turn a picture into evidence of your learning. A strong caption has three parts.

  • Name the medium or project (for example, "Surrealist photomontage, Module 19").
  • Name at least one specific Element of Art or Principle of Design the piece demonstrates (for example, "uses contrast between warm and cool color to direct the eye").
  • One sentence on what you learned making it. Not "I liked this one," but something specific: what changed in your thinking or skill.

Presentation is how you package the whole collection. Build your portfolio as a simple, clean document: an M365 Word document or PowerPoint slideshow, with one piece per page or slide, the image and its caption placed together. Use the same layout, font, and image size on every page so the portfolio reads as one finished piece of work, not a pile of leftovers.

🎨 CONSISTENT PRESENTATION
Crop or resize every image to a similar dimension and place it in the same spot on each page or slide. Use one background color and one caption style throughout. A portfolio with mismatched sizes, random placement, and inconsistent fonts reads as unfinished, even if the art inside it is strong.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · WRITE YOUR OWN CAPTIONS
An AI tool can write a generic caption for any image in seconds, but it was not there when you made the piece and does not know what you learned. A portfolio is evidence of your growth and thinking. A caption you did not actually think through defeats the purpose of the assignment, even if the sentence reads smoothly.
⚡ TRY IT · STRONG CAPTION OR WEAK CAPTION?
Read each caption, decide, then check your answer.
"Photomontage, Module 17. I combined three photos using layer masks to create visual movement across the frame, and I learned how much a soft-edged mask can blend two images without a hard seam."
"This is one of my favorites from this year. I think it turned out really cool."

💪 THE BIG IDEA

In Module 1 you learned that the artist behind the tool is what matters, not the software itself. Your portfolio is the proof. Every selection, every ordering decision, and every caption is a choice you made on purpose, the same kind of choice you have been practicing all year. Seen together, this collection is not just a folder of files; it is evidence of who you have become as a digital artist.
