📚 PART 2 · QUARTER 1 RECAP: FOUNDATIONS & PHOTOGRAPHY

Quarter 1 gave you the vocabulary and the eye you needed before you ever touched an editing tool. In brief:

  • Digital art vs. digital imaging. Digital art uses technology at any stage; digital imaging is art made to be viewed on a screen.
  • Pixels, resolution, raster vs. vector. Pixels are the tiny squares that build a screen image; resolution is how many you have. Raster images (photos, Pixlr E files) are pixel grids; vector images are math-defined lines that stay crisp at any size.
  • Elements of Art & Principles of Design. The visual vocabulary, line, shape, color, texture, space, and how they get arranged, balance, emphasis, movement, rhythm, that describes every image you make or study.
  • The color wheel. Warm, cool, neutral, monochromatic, and polychromatic color schemes, plus how to build color deliberately on a digital canvas.
  • Shape and pen tools. Building and combining shapes as the foundation of digital drawing.
  • Line and space in photography. How line and space guide a viewer's eye through a composed photograph.
  • History of photography and taking a strong photo. From the camera obscura to phones, plus the habits, framing, focus, lighting, that make a photo intentional instead of accidental.
  • Studying photographers. Looking closely at real photographers' choices and building your own shot list before you shoot.

🔍 PART 3 · QUARTER 2 RECAP: CAPTURING LIGHT & EDITING

Quarter 2 moved from taking the photo to editing it with control. In brief:

  • Lighting techniques and photo series. Using lighting (backlighting, side lighting, diffused light) and building a photo series with a unifying theme: subject, compositional technique, movement, texture, or color.
  • Non-destructive editing workflow. Always duplicate your original layer before editing, and do not overedit; extreme adjustments make an image look unnatural. Layers let you experiment without losing your original.
  • The Crop tool. Cutting out unwanted parts of an image, changing the balance and focus of the composition, and choosing an aspect ratio that suits the final use.
  • Exposure correction. Brightness/Contrast adjusts highlights and the light-to-dark range; Exposure changes the overall light; Shadows/Highlights targets only the darkest or brightest pixels instead of the whole image.
  • Color adjustment. Hue/Saturation changes color, intensity, and lightness; Color Balance shifts shadows, midtones, and highlights; Vibrance boosts dull colors without blowing out ones that are already bright.
  • Filters. Using a blur filter, such as Gaussian blur, on a separated background layer to emphasize your subject, the digital version of a wide-aperture blur.
  • The photo series capstone. Bringing lighting, composition, and editing together into one finished, intentional series.
💡 STUDY TIP
For the quiz, do not just reread definitions. Open Pixlr E and find each tool as you review it: Crop, Brightness/Contrast, Exposure, Shadows/Highlights, Hue/Saturation, Color Balance, Vibrance, and a blur filter. Naming a tool while you look at it sticks better than reading its name on a page.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI IMAGE TOOLS
An AI tool can guess a crop or auto-adjust exposure and color in one click. It cannot tell you why a photographer chose to leave shadows dark on purpose, or which crop actually serves your composition's balance and emphasis. Those judgment calls, the ones this semester trained you to make, are what the quiz and discussion this week are checking.
📝 VOCABULARY · SEMESTER 1
PixelOne of the tiny colored squares that make up a screen image.
Raster ImageAn image built from a fixed grid of pixels, like a photograph.
Vector ImageAn image built from math-defined lines and shapes; stays crisp at any size.
Elements of ArtThe visual vocabulary of art: line, shape, color, texture, space, form, value.
Color WheelThe circular map of color relationships used to build warm, cool, neutral, monochromatic, and polychromatic schemes.
Non-Destructive EditingEditing on a duplicate layer so your original photo is never permanently changed.
ExposureThe overall amount of light captured or shown in an image.
Shadows/HighlightsAn adjustment tool that targets only the darkest or brightest pixels in a photo.
VibranceAn adjustment that boosts dull colors without over-saturating colors that are already bright.
Photo SeriesA set of images connected by a shared theme, subject, technique, or color.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Foundations, composition, photography, and editing are not four separate units, they are one skill practiced in stages. You learned what an image is made of, how to arrange it, how to capture it with a camera, and how to refine it afterward with intention instead of guesswork. Every stage still lives inside every image you make from here. Prove what you know this week, then carry all four stages into Quarter 3.
