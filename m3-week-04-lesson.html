Studio / Critique Week
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What separates a finished piece from a draft?
You already built the concept and the bones of your Surrealism Collage last week. This week is studio time: look at it again with fresh eyes, hear from a classmate, and finish it with care. This is a lighter lesson; most of your time goes to your own work.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST: SELF-CRITIQUE

Before anyone else looks at your collage, look at it yourself, honestly. Open your Week 3 file in Pixlr E, zoom out so you can see the whole composition, and walk through this checklist.

  • Is my Surrealist concept clear at a glance, or does the viewer need it explained?
  • Do my masked edges look intentional (either smooth or deliberately visible), or are there stray hard edges I did not mean to leave?
  • Does my blend mode actually help the elements feel like they belong together?
  • Does my balance (symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial) still hold up, or has the composition gotten lopsided as I added pieces?
  • Is my file free of stray leftover layers, duplicate elements, or unintentional gaps?
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which part of your collage are you most likely to revise this week?
📚 PART 2 · PEER CRITIQUE

A critique is a detailed, thoughtful evaluation of a work of art, not a simple "I like it" or "I don't." Sharing your collage with a classmate this week gives you a second set of eyes: they can see things about your composition you have stared at too long to notice.

Giving feedback

Describe what you see before you judge it. Name one part that works well and why. Ask one honest question about a part that confuses you.

Receiving feedback

Listen without defending your choices right away. You do not have to change everything a classmate suggests; you do have to consider it seriously.

💡 A GOOD CRITIQUE COMMENT HAS THREE PARTS
1. What I see: describe the concept and composition specifically, not just "cool" or "weird." 2. What works: name one choice, a mask, a blend mode, a balance decision, that is doing real work. 3. One question: ask about something unclear or offer one specific idea for a stronger finish.
🔍 PART 3 · FINISHING AND YOUR PORTFOLIO

Once you have made your revisions, finish the piece properly:

  • Flatten your layers only after you are satisfied; flattening makes further layer edits harder.
  • Export as a PNG at full resolution.
  • Save the final file to your OneDrive portfolio folder using this course's naming convention.

Keeping a clean, well-named portfolio folder matters beyond this course: it is the same habit working digital artists use to manage client files and personal projects.

⚡ TRY IT · CRITIQUE OR NOT?
Read each comment, decide if it is a real critique, then check your answer.
"Your masked edge around the fish is really clean, it looks like it belongs in the scene. Did you plan the blend mode on the water layer, or find it by testing?"
"This is so cool!"
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI FEEDBACK TOOLS
An AI tool can generate generic feedback about a composition in seconds, but it has not seen your concept develop and cannot tell you whether your specific idea reads clearly to a real viewer. This week's peer critique is graded on the quality of a genuine exchange between two students, not a generated comment.
📝 VOCABULARY
CritiqueA detailed evaluation or judgment of a work of art.
Self-CritiqueEvaluating your own work honestly before sharing or submitting it.
RevisionA deliberate change made to improve a piece after a first draft.
FlattenMerging all layers into a single image, usually done near final export.
PortfolioAn organized collection of an artist's work, saved and named consistently.
CraftsmanshipThe care and precision shown in finishing a piece of work.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

A finished piece is not the moment you stop working; it is the moment you have looked hard enough, and listened well enough, to know the work says what you meant it to say. Take the time this week to make that true of your collage.
