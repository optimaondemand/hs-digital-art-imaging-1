📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
How do the direction of light and the angle of the camera change what a photo says about its subject?
The word "photography" literally means "drawing with light." Before you press the shutter, two decisions matter more than almost anything else: where the light is coming from, and where you are standing. This week you learn to control both on purpose.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

A light source is where the light in a photo is coming from: the sun, a lamp, a window. Sometimes it is obvious in the photo; sometimes you have to look at the shadows to find it. The same subject, lit from three different directions, produces three very different photos.

📸 PART 2 · LIGHTING IN PHOTOGRAPHY

Poor lighting can make even the best subject look dull or unclear, so learning to read and use light is one of the most valuable skills in photography. Two things to always notice: the type of light (natural or artificial) and its direction.

Front lighting

Light comes from in front of the subject. Highlights the subject and reduces shadows, but can look flat and is not always best for landscapes or scenes that need depth. If the sun is the source, your subject might squint.

Side lighting

Light hits the subject from the side, creating shadows on the opposite side. Adds dimension and depth, which is great for portraits and animals.

Back lighting comes from behind the subject and can create silhouettes if the subject blocks the light. Details may be hard to see, but the effect can be dramatic.

Time of day changes light dramatically. During golden hour (the hour after sunrise or before sunset), the sun sits low and produces soft, golden light with vibrant colors and gentle shadows. During blue hour (the hour before sunrise or after sunset), the sun is below the horizon and gives a cool, bluish light well suited to moody, atmospheric photos.

Weather matters too: cloudy skies give soft, even light with fewer harsh shadows; sunny days give bright, strong light with high contrast; rain or fog adds mood through reflections and muted color; snow reflects light and can brighten a scene with a crisp, clean look.

💡 BEFORE YOU SHOOT
Walk around your subject before you take the photo. Notice where the light is coming from and how the shadows fall from each angle. Pick the direction that matches what you want the photo to say, calm and even, dramatic and deep, or mysterious and dark.
📍 PART 3 · PERSPECTIVE

Most photos are taken from eye level. Simply changing your position, or the angle of your device, can completely transform an image. Perspective is your other major tool alongside light.

Bird's-eye view (from above)
Worm's-eye view (from below)
One-point perspective

A bird's-eye view photographs the subject from above, as if looking down from the sky. You can create this from a balcony, a staircase, a ladder, or a safe high point, or with a drone. Always prioritize safety and secure your device with a strap or case.

A worm's-eye view is the opposite: you photograph from below, looking up as if you were on the ground. Try lying down, sitting or kneeling, or lowering your device close to the ground. This viewpoint makes the subject appear larger, more dramatic, and sometimes heroic.

One-point perspective happens when lines in a scene, like a road, hallway, or row of trees, appear to converge toward a single point in the distance. Shooting straight down a long, symmetrical space is the easiest way to find it.

Before you look: which perspective (bird's-eye or worm's-eye) would make a skateboarder mid-jump look most heroic? Decide your answer, then click to check.
Worm's-eye view. Shooting from low, looking up, makes the skateboarder appear larger and more dramatic against the sky, which is why sports and action photographers use this angle so often.

Forced perspective is a creative technique that intentionally tricks the viewer's eye into thinking objects are bigger, smaller, closer, or farther than they really are, for example lining up a foreground object and a background object so they appear on the same plane, like someone "holding" the moon. It takes planning and experimentation but can produce unforgettable images.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI IMAGE TOOLS
An AI image generator can produce a picture that looks like a bird's-eye view or a golden-hour photo from a text prompt. It cannot climb the staircase, lie down on the sidewalk, or wait for the actual sun to drop to the right angle. Those physical decisions, made with your own camera, are what this week's project grades.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Light and position are the two decisions you make before you ever touch an editing tool. Where the light falls decides the mood; where you stand decides the story. Master both and half your photo is already finished before you open Pixlr E.
