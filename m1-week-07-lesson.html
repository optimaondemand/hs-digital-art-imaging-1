📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
Where does a photographer find a great subject when nothing "interesting" seems to be around?
You do not need a rare object or a dramatic location to make strong art. You need line, space, and a technique, applied to something ordinary. This week you build a shot list from everyday categories so you walk into next week's capstone with a plan instead of a blank page.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

This week's goal, in one sentence: take a digital photograph of an ordinary object and transform it into something extraordinary using line, space, one photography technique, and one compositional technique from this unit. That is the exact assignment you will complete next week, so treat today as scouting, not shooting.

School Objects

Pencils, notebooks, scissors, paint tubes. One example filled the whole frame with paint tubes to remove distractions, then angled them to create leading lines through the image.

Dishes & Kitchen Items

A cup, bowl, or plate. One example placed a cup off-center using the rule of thirds, then shot through the handle of another mug to frame it.

Furniture

Chairs, tables, anything you sit on or near. One example used a low, unusual angle to highlight leading lines formed by rows of chairs and tables.

Accessories

Sunglasses, headphones, belts, hats. One example shot an umbrella from underneath, letting its radiating spokes become leading lines pulling the eye toward the center.

Reflective Surfaces

Mirrors, glasses, windows, water. One example used a glass sphere to reflect a field of flowers upside-down, letting the sphere itself frame the reflection while the background stayed blurred.

📚 PART 2 · READING A PHOTOGRAPHER'S CHOICES

Notice that none of the five examples above relied on an exotic subject. Every one of them combined an ordinary object with a deliberate technique from earlier this module:

  • Leading lines (the paint tubes, the chair rows, the umbrella spokes) guide the eye through the frame.
  • Rule of thirds (the off-center cup) makes a simple object feel composed rather than accidental.
  • Framing (shooting through the mug handle, or inside the glass sphere) uses one object to draw attention to another.
  • Depth of field (the blurred background behind the glass sphere) is a camera technique that isolates the subject through exclusion.

This is the pattern behind almost every strong photograph of an ordinary object: one clear subject, one line-based composition choice, and one technique that removes or reduces everything competing for attention.

💡 STUDYING PHOTOGRAPHERS ON YOUR OWN
Many working photographers post their process online. If you want to see more examples at a larger scale, search a photography category that interests you (still life, street, nature) on a museum or gallery site rather than a general image search, since museum sites credit the photographer and the year. Living and recent photographers' work belongs to them: view and study it on their own site or a museum's site rather than downloading or reposting it.
🔍 PART 3 · BUILD YOUR SHOT LIST

Before next week's project, scout three possible ordinary objects and plan one technique for each. A shot list turns "I'll figure it out later" into a plan you can execute in ten minutes.

  1. Object 1: ______________ · Line type or technique to try: ______________
  2. Object 2: ______________ · Line type or technique to try: ______________
  3. Object 3: ______________ · Line type or technique to try: ______________

You do not have to shoot all three. Having three planned gives you a backup if your first idea does not work once you are looking through the lens.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI IMAGE SEARCH
An AI tool can generate example images of "creative still life photography" in seconds, but those images are not real photographs and cannot substitute for studying an actual photographer's choices. When you research this week, look at real photographs by real photographers on museum or gallery sites, where the composition decisions were made by a person working with an actual camera and an actual object.
📝 VOCABULARY
Shot ListA planned list of subjects and techniques to try before shooting.
FramingUsing one object or shape in a photo to surround and draw attention to another.
Depth of FieldA camera technique that controls how much of the image is in sharp focus, often used to blur a background.
Leading LineA line within a photograph that guides the viewer's eye toward a subject or into the distance.
Rule of ThirdsDividing a frame into nine equal parts to place a subject off-center, near a grid intersection.
Ordinary ObjectA common, everyday item used as the subject of an intentionally composed photograph.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Inspiration is not a rare object. It is an ordinary object plus a deliberate technique. Every example this week started with something you already own: a mug, an umbrella, a chair. What made each photograph extraordinary was a photographer who planned a line, decided what to include and exclude, and pressed the shutter on purpose.
