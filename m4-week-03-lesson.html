📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
What does a piece of art need before it is actually finished?
This is a studio module: less new reading, more time in Pixlr E. You will finish the mosaic you started in Module 26, look at your own work with a critical eye, hear from a classmate, and get it portfolio-ready.
👀 PART 1 · STUDIO TIME: FINISH YOUR MOSAIC

Open your mosaic file from Module 26 and finish placing tiles across the full canvas. Before you call it done, check it against this list.

  • Every part of the canvas is covered with tesserae; no blank or unfinished gaps remain.
  • Tile size is consistent across the piece (unless you chose a deliberate size change for effect).
  • Seams or gaps between tiles are visible throughout, not just in some areas.
  • Stepping back (zoom out or shrink the window), the subject reads clearly as one image.
  • Color choices are deliberate: you can say why you picked each tile color, not just "it looked fine."
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Where are you in your mosaic right now?
📚 PART 2 · SELF-CRITIQUE

A critique looks at a piece of art in four steps: describe what is there, analyze how the elements work, interpret what it communicates, and judge whether it succeeds. Before anyone else sees your mosaic, walk through these four steps yourself.

  • Describe: What is the subject? What colors and tile scale did you use?
  • Analyze: How do the elements of art (color, shape, value) work together in this piece?
  • Interpret: What mood or idea does the piece communicate, even without words?
  • Judge: Does it succeed as a mosaic specifically, not just as a picture? Would it still read clearly from across a room?
💡 ONE HONEST FIX
Self-critique is only useful if it changes something. Find one specific area of your mosaic that is weaker than the rest, whether that is a muddy color transition, an inconsistent tile size, or a spot where the seams disappear, and fix that one area before you submit.
🔍 PART 3 · PEER CRITIQUE AND EXPORT

This module's discussion asks you to give and receive critique using the same four steps: describe, analyze, interpret, judge. Specific, respectful feedback is a skill, not a talent. Naming exactly what you see, using art vocabulary, is more useful to a classmate than a general "I like it" or "it's okay."

⚡ TRY IT · STRONG OR WEAK CRITIQUE?
Read each critique comment and decide whether it follows the describe/analyze/interpret/judge model well.
"This looks cool, good job."
"Your coarse tile grid on the mountain reads as abstract and bold, but the sky's fine grid feels smoother by comparison; from a distance the contrast actually helps the mountain stand out."

Once your mosaic is finished and critiqued, export it: File > Export as PNG. Save a copy to your portfolio folder. You will draw on this same portfolio folder for your Final Project and Final Portfolio later this quarter, so keep filenames consistent now.

📝 VOCABULARY
CritiqueA structured look at a piece of art through describing, analyzing, interpreting, and judging it.
DescribeThe first critique step: stating plainly what is visible in the work.
AnalyzeThe critique step that examines how the elements of art work together.
InterpretThe critique step that considers what mood or idea the work communicates.
JudgeThe critique step that evaluates whether the work succeeds, with reasoning.
Self-CritiqueApplying the critique process to your own unfinished or finished work.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Finishing is its own skill, separate from starting. A critical, honest look at your own work, followed by one specific person's honest look, catches problems you stop seeing after hours of staring at the same file. That habit will serve you again in Module 31, when you critique and finish your final project.
